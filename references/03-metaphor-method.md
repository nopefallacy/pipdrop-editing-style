# 03 · The metaphor method

The style lives or dies on choosing the *precise* image. Process:

## 1. Read the whole story, then find the turn
Print the transcript as `word@time` lines and read it end to end before choosing anything. Name:
- **The turn:** the sentence where the meaning flips ("the only reason… is to help my mom"; "trust your own instinct").
- **The arc** in 2-4 acts, for example *want → dig → answer* or *bond → fall → repair*.
- **The answer:** the one moment that gets the accent colour.

## 2. Pick a visual family per act
Each act gets its own source family, so the film reads as chapters, not as a slideshow:
- **Inner life:** fine-art paintings, mostly 1300-1900 (public domain, emotionally loaded, never look like stock).
- **Real life:** the speaker's own photos and clips (only if they have them and want them).
- **Plain metaphors:** clean photos (spotlight, maze, burnt match, kintsugi, compass, lighthouse).
- **Ideas:** a drawn diagram (bars, a rising line, a layered iceberg).

A myth thread can carry a whole film. Icarus worked for a mentor story: Daedalus fitting the wings ("I had a dream"),
then the Fall of Icarus ("I lost my trust in him"), with the mentor still flying above him.

## 3. Write the beat list
`time · phrase · emotion · image idea · layout`, one line per beat. For each phrase ask:
1. What does the speaker *feel* here, not just what do they say?
2. Is there an image a stranger would understand in under a second?
3. Does it belong to *this* story's context? If the literal subject is someone else's (a webinar host, a brand), use the face.
4. Is it stronger as a detail crop (`zoom`)?
5. Which layout does the beat want: single (card), immersive (full), several things (stack), everything (collage),
   stepping back (pip) or re-placing the speaker (green)?

## 4. One recurring device
Something that comes back changed carries the argument better than ten new pictures:
- **Iceberg:** "to be seen" above the water; "to help people" below; descend on "going deeper"; at the bottom, in gold, the real why.
  It's a real iceberg photo, extended with sky and deep-water gradients, with serif labels and a thin plumb line. Visit it 3 times.
- **Bars:** "who I trusted: him ███████ / me ▏" → "who I trust now: him ██▏ / me ███████ (gold)".
- Others that fit the grammar: a ladder of "why?" steps, a scale that tips, a door ajar → open.

## 5. Leave the face where it matters
On the face: the hook's first second, confessions ("I really don't know how I can serve them back"), direct questions to the viewer,
and the line right before the payoff (a slow push-in instead). Punch-ins carry long face stretches.

## Avoid
- Images whose context is someone else's story (see rule 2 in SKILL.md).
- Nudity at the centre of the frame, even in classical paintings (the platform's reach and the viewer's focus both suffer).
  Draper's *Lament for Icarus* was swapped for Gowy's *Fall of Icarus* for this reason.
- AI-looking or watermarked stock, generic sad faces, icons, emojis, clip-art.
- Two images in one beat unless it's a deliberate stack or collage.
